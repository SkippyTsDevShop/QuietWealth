The New ETFs Investors Should Know About This Year

2026 is on pace for a record year of ETF launches. Most won't matter to you. A handful — in AI infrastructure, securitized credit, and stablecoin-compliant cash management — actually might.

QuietWealth Staff September 15, 2026 8 min read

Fund issuers have launched over a thousand new ETFs in 2026, putting the industry on pace for a record year. The overwhelming majority of them are narrow, thinly traded, and built to capture a fleeting trend rather than serve as anything you'd hold for a decade. But a small number of this year's launches reflect something more durable — real shifts in what kind of exposure investors are asking for. Four are worth understanding, whether or not you ever buy them.

AI infrastructure, one layer down from the obvious names

Most "AI ETFs" launched over the past few years have leaned on the same handful of well-known chipmakers and cloud platforms. One of this year's most successful new launches took a different angle: a fund from Roundhill Investments built around the high-bandwidth memory chip industry — the specialized memory hardware that sits alongside AI processors and has become a bottleneck in its own right. Its holdings lean heavily on a small number of memory manufacturers, which makes it a concentrated bet on one specific layer of the AI hardware stack rather than a diversified AI holding. It gathered billions in assets within months of launching, which tells you more about investor appetite for anything AI-adjacent than it does about the fund's long-term durability.

Space, riding pre-IPO anticipation

Space-themed ETFs aren't new, but 2026 saw seven of them launch in the first half of the year alone — a sign of how much retail interest has built around commercial space. The largest of the new crop, from Tema ETFs, drew attention for including indirect, structured exposure to a major private space company ahead of a widely anticipated IPO. That structure is worth understanding before investing in any fund like it: exposure to a private company through a special-purpose vehicle inside an ETF wrapper behaves differently than owning a normal public stock, with different liquidity and valuation dynamics.

Securitized credit, going active

Away from the thematic, headline-grabbing launches, one of the more structurally interesting new funds this year came from iShares: an actively managed fund spreading exposure across commercial mortgages, non-agency mortgage-backed securities, and collateralized loan obligations, with a yield meaningfully above what most core bond funds are currently offering. It converted from an existing mutual fund with a two-decade track record rather than launching from scratch — a reminder that not every "new" ETF is actually a new strategy; some are simply an established approach moving into a more tax-efficient, more liquid wrapper.

Stablecoin-compliant cash management

The most quietly significant launch of the year, in terms of what it signals rather than its size, is a money market fund from ProShares built specifically to meet the reserve requirements laid out under the GENIUS Act for stablecoin issuers — a stricter standard than the rules governing typical money market funds. It gathered over $20 billion in assets within months, largely through internal allocations rather than new-to-market money, but the underlying signal matters: regulated fund managers are now building products explicitly designed around stablecoin infrastructure, not adjacent to it.

Worth Watching

That signal extends beyond fund managers. As stablecoin-compliant financial products move from novelty to standard infrastructure, payment platforms such as Switch are positioning themselves around a related but distinct trend: making everyday crypto-to-cash conversion and cross-border remittances simpler for ordinary users. It's an early-stage space and a different layer of the stack than the ETF market — worth watching as the regulatory groundwork for stablecoins keeps solidifying.

What to actually do with any of this

New doesn't mean necessary. A thematic ETF launched on genuine enthusiasm can still be a reasonable small satellite position — or it can be a concentrated bet dressed up as diversification. Before adding any new fund to a portfolio, it's worth checking three things a flashy launch headline never tells you: how concentrated the underlying holdings actually are, what the expense ratio costs you every year regardless of performance, and whether the fund has enough trading volume that you could exit it without moving the price yourself.

The boring, structural launches — like the securitized credit conversion or the stablecoin-compliant money market fund — often matter more to a long-term portfolio than the exciting thematic ones, even though they'll never trend on social media the same way.
